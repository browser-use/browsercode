Delegate one short, obvious browser state transition to a cheap Browser Use leaf agent.

Use this tool only when:
- the current operation is narrow and browser-only;
- the stopping condition is directly observable on the page;
- roughly eight or fewer model steps should be sufficient;
- the parent can verify the resulting page afterward.

Good delegations include filling a bounded form, applying known filters,
navigating to a known record, or revealing a known result. Do not delegate
broad research, exhaustive scraping, final-answer composition, filesystem
work, or ambiguous interpretation.

MUST-DELEGATE RULE: once the starting page and intended control are known, if
your next browser action would click, type into, or select a known UI control
and the result has an observable stopping state, call browser_delegate now.
Do this BEFORE browser_execute performs that mutation. browser_execute may
inspect the page, select the correct tab, or navigate to the starting page, but
must not perform a qualifying interaction itself. This rule still applies
after you already spent time discovering the control.

If the user explicitly asks to fill a form, apply a named filter, choose named
options, or open a named record and identifies the website, the requested
transition is already known from the task. Call browser_delegate as the first
browser tool and include the starting URL in its task; the leaf can navigate
there. Do not first load browser-execute, search for the control,
reverse-engineer its URL, or replace the requested UI operation with scraping.
A task may contain a delegated transition followed by parent-owned extraction:
perform the transition first, verify it, then do the extraction yourself.

Example: after locating a "Low-Carb" filter, delegate applying that filter and
waiting for filtered results. Then use browser_execute to verify the filter and
extract the recipe titles. Do not click the filter or open its filtered URL
yourself.

Do not save this tool as a last-ditch retry after many manual attempts. If the
leaf gives up, take over with browser_execute; do not repeatedly delegate the
same transition.

The leaf agent knows it is a subagent and may give up. Its completion status is
only a claim. After this tool returns, ALWAYS use browser_execute to inspect the
browser and independently verify done_when before relying on the result.
